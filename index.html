The cheat sheet

What the levels mean

Five lines, in plain English.

♛ King

The magnet

The biggest gamma strike. Price tends to get pulled toward it and pinned there.

Heads up: the King can sit on the same strike as the Call Wall sometimes. The chart labels it "King · Call Wall".
Call Wall

The ceiling

Biggest positive gamma above price, where rallies tend to stall.

Put Wall

The floor

Most negative gamma below price, where selloffs tend to find support.

Chud Node

The speed bump

The next biggest node above or below price. Moves tend to pause here.

Flip

The mood switch

Where gamma turns from positive to negative. Above it, moves tend to stay slow and pinned. Below it, moves tend to speed up.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Is this financial advice?

No. Money Seeker shows levels calculated from public options data for educational purposes. It does not tell you what to buy or sell. Always do your own research and manage your own risk.

What is GEX (gamma exposure)?

GEX estimates how much options dealers may need to hedge as price moves, strike by strike. Big positive gamma strikes tend to act like magnets or ceilings; negative gamma areas tend to let moves run faster.

Which tickers are supported?

SPY, SPX, QQQ and most optionable stocks and ETFs. Type a ticker into the app, pick an expiration, and the levels load on the chart.

Why are the King and Call Wall sometimes the same line?

The King is the single biggest gamma strike; the Call Wall is the biggest positive strike above price. When those land on the same strike, the chart shows one combined "King · Call Wall" line.
